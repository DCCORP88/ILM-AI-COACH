## 1. Readiness (before anything else)
- **Deanna's rule (confirmed 16 Nov 2026):** you're ready when you're confident, can answer any question a student throws at you, and can **fix problems, not just perform the service**. It isn't a set number of years.
- **The money test:** if it's only for the money, rethink. Every certificate carries your name, and a poorly trained graduate damages your reputation.
- Common fears: "Am I experienced enough?" You need skill and proof of results, not 20 years. "What if I can't answer?" Be honest, find out, follow up.
- Choose your educator type: **skill-based, business-based or hybrid** (Deanna calls hybrid the highest-value model).
- Start small: 1–2 students or a beta group, collect proof, then grow.

## 2. Validate demand before building
- Signals: people already ask you; competitors sell similar courses; people already pay to solve the problem.
- **Six questions** for potential students: biggest challenge · how they solve it now · what success looks like · what they'd pay · preferred format · what's stopped them before.
- Red flags: they only want free; saturated with no gap; audience too narrow; trend-only interest.
- **Ideal Student Profile:** who they are / what stops them / what they want. "Write their words, not yours."

## 3. Design the course
- **Course in how to teach vs what to teach:** the educator brings the craft; the course structure makes it teachable and assessable.
- **Scope:** "Your first course doesn't need everything you know. It needs what your student needs next." Sort topics into **must / should / could**. Must-know becomes the sessions; could-know waits for a later course.
- **Outcomes first:** what students will be able to *do* by the end (SMART / ABCD).
- **Structure:** 5–8 modules is the sweet spot. Use layered learning: theory → demo → supervised practice → feedback → assessed case studies.
- **Format:**
  - **In person:** lash extension courses need a minimum of 2 days (content, full set, refill and removal won't fit in one). 1-day courses are fine for lash lift and brows. Plan models, kits, venue, class size.
  - **Online:** decide self-paced vs live; plan how practical skill is checked remotely (photo or video submissions, or a practical day); platform; access period; student support.
  - **Hybrid:** theory online, practical in person. Deanna: hybrid suits roughly $800–$2,500 (*her figure, verify*).
- **Assessment:** every assessment checks a named outcome. Typical: theory quiz with a pass mark, practical sets or case studies against written criteria, and a clear resit path. "Completion isn't competence. A student who isn't ready gets a practice plan and a resit, not a certificate."
- **Never call it "accredited" or "certified"** unless you can name the accrediting body (COACH_INSTRUCTIONS §10).

## 4. Price the course
- **Cost it before you price it:** kits, venue, models, materials, platform fees, marketing, insurance, prep and admin hours, payment-plan fees ÷ realistic students. "A copied price can mean paying to teach." Use the pricing worksheet; never guess.
- **Price on transformation, not time.** "Underpricing is not humility. It is a marketing problem."
- **Positioning:** beginner mentor, advanced expert or niche specialist. Deanna's ILM course is positioned as **a career shift, not a skill course**, which justifies its price.
- Payment plans and deposits widen access, so include their fees in the price. Don't haggle.

## 5. What makes a course sell (Deanna)
1. **A clear offer.** Who it's for, what they'll be able to do, why now, why you (the 4 Clarity Questions). "I help [WHO] to [DO WHAT] so they can [OUTCOME]."
2. **Modules aren't marketing.** Students assume the basics are covered. Before listing modules, say **who it's for, what they'll do by the end, and how you'll assess them**. "Show your standard, not your syllabus." Say how much practice they get, on how many models, and when they get feedback.
3. **Trust and proof.** Student results with specific numbers, before/afters, your standards. "Authority is about being trusted, not being seen." Show *how you teach*, not just finished sets.
4. **A warmed audience.** "Launch day is not day one." People who've been taught by your content and emails buy; cold strangers rarely do.
5. **Objections answered with proof, not discounts.** Money ("I don't see the return"), time ("I don't trust I'll follow through"), trust ("will it work for me?"), timing ("maybe later"). Never discount to answer a trust objection.
6. **Honest urgency.** A real deadline: limited seats, a set intake date, a founding price that genuinely ends. "A deadline with no case behind it isn't urgency. It's a coupon."

## 6. How to launch (Deanna's Launch & Sell order)
**Four weeks, in this order:**
1. **Build the systems:** a lead magnet that solves a smaller version of the same problem → sign-up form with a tag → nurture emails → comment-to-DM automation. Test the whole chain end to end.
2. **Warm your list first** (weeks 4–3 out): email and DM people who already know you.
3. **Public content and waitlist** (weeks 3–1 out). Week 1 names the problem, week 2 teaches, week 3 shares proof. The waitlist opens before the cart.
4. **Cart opens:** waitlist first (early bird), then everyone.

**Landing page, four sections:** outcome headline → the exact problem → the offer as numbered steps → proof with specific numbers, then a repeated call to action. Trust comes before price.

**Launch emails, four in order:** Open (AIDA) → Value (FAB: "12 modules, meaning you can…") → Objections (PAS) → Last call (one deadline, name whether seats or price are ending, nothing new). Every email gets one proof point and one outcome-worded call to action.

**After the launch:**
- Confirm buyers within the hour, with no upsell.
- Send non-buyers a clean close, not a guilt trip.
- Review within a week: where interest dropped, what people said, the real objections.
- Re-list interested non-buyers for the next intake.
- "A launch that doesn't sell is data. Only a launch nobody reviews is wasted." If nobody buys, diagnose the offer, audience or trust gap before touching price.

**Collect proof continuously:** ask right after a student's result, prompt for a number, confirm permission, store it in one place.

## How the coach uses this
- Use the activity's intake answers. Course format (in person / online / hybrid), topic and learner carry across the educator activities.
- **Readiness:** be honest. If they can't yet fix problems or answer hard questions, the plan closes those gaps first.
- **Design:** produce the course outline (outcomes, must/should/could, modules and lessons, assessment plan, materials), matched to the format.
- **Price:** run the worksheet with their cost lines and student numbers, then check positioning.
- **Launch:** build a dated 4-week plan from their launch date, audience size and list size. Draft emails and landing-page copy as **drafts only**. Nothing is sent or published.
- Never promise enrolments or income. Never use "accredited" or "certified" without a named body.
- [GENERAL GUIDANCE] Consumer law applies to course terms (refunds, payment plans). Recommend clear written terms, reviewed for their jurisdiction.
