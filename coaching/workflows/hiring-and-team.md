## 1. When it's time to hire (Deanna's signs)

- Your **books are full** and you can't fit clients in.
- You need someone **available at times you can't cover**.
- You're working **ridiculous hours** to get everyone in, and **burning out** as a solo artist.
- You have **no time to work *on* your business** because you're always working *in* it.

> If you're always in the chair, nobody is running the business.

## 2. How Deanna hires

**Trial service first.** Lash'd assesses candidates on a trial service and looks at:
- **how they perform the trial service**: technique, hygiene, how they manage the client
- **whether they're personable**: how they talk to the client
- **how they fit with the team**
- **whether they're willing to learn**
- **whether they love the industry**

> Skills can be trained. Attitude, personality and love of the industry are much harder to teach.

## 3. How Deanna pays

- **Base wage**
- **plus commission when they hit KPIs**
- **plus commission on product sales** (which also ties into retail; see COACH_INSTRUCTIONS §9)

**[GENERAL GUIDANCE]** On how much staff should cost:
- Track total staff costs (wages, commission, super, leave) as a percentage of revenue every month, so you know whether a new hire pays for themselves.
- There's no single right percentage. It depends on your prices, services and how much you work in the business yourself. Before hiring, work through the numbers with your accountant: what extra revenue a new staff member must bring in to cover their full cost.
- The coach uses the **pricing worksheet** to model this and never quotes an "industry standard" percentage as fact.

## 4. Onboarding

- **SOPs**: new staff learn the Lash'd way from written standard operating procedures.
- **Clients straight away, at a cheaper rate.** New staff start taking clients immediately at a reduced price. This builds their confidence, and clients know what they're getting.

**[GENERAL GUIDANCE]** Additional steps Deanna may want to approve:
- First week: walk through the SOPs, policies (deposits, cancellations, rebooking), Fresha, and the products used.
- Set clear criteria for moving from the reduced rate to full price (e.g. after X clients, or once retention and quality checks are met).
- Agree KPIs in writing from day one, so commission is clear and fair.

## 5. Keeping the team consistent — [GENERAL GUIDANCE, Deanna to review]

Deanna didn't have a set answer here. Proposed practices:
- **SOPs for every service and process** (setup, consultation, aftercare talk, rebooking, retail, cleaning). Update them whenever something changes.
- **Regular short team meetings** (e.g. weekly 15–20 minutes): bookings, KPIs, client feedback, one skill or standard to focus on.
- **Spot checks**: review photos of finished work and client feedback, and give feedback privately and promptly.
- **Ongoing training**: Deanna's courses stress continuing education ("stay the student").
- **Make the standard visible**: share KPI and rebooking rates with the team so everyone can see how they're tracking.

## 6. The biggest mistake (Deanna)

> Getting too comfortable with staff, to the point where they don't respect you.

Be warm, but you are the business owner, not one of the team. Friendliness doesn't replace clear expectations, KPIs and following through on standards.

## How the coach uses this

**Typical triggers:** "I'm burnt out", "should I hire someone", "how do I find a good lash artist", "how should I pay staff", "my staff don't follow my standards".

**Check first** (profile/project; ask only what's missing):
1. Current capacity: how full the books are, hours worked, waitlist.
2. Revenue and costs, enough to know whether a hire is affordable (use the worksheet; never guess).
3. What role they need (fully trained artist, junior, receptionist, VA).
4. Whether SOPs exist yet.
5. Jurisdiction, for employment law.

**Then:**
- Confirm whether the signs in §1 apply. If capacity isn't the problem, hiring may not be the answer: look at pricing or systems first.
- Help them plan the trial, the assessment criteria, the pay structure and the onboarding.
- If there are no SOPs, start writing them **before** the hire.
- **Next action:** e.g. write the role description and trial checklist this week. **Measure:** hours the owner works in the chair, bookings fitted in, and the new staff member's rebooking rate after 8 weeks.

**Limits (important):**
- **Employment law** governs wages, awards, super, contracts, leave, and whether a **trial shift must be paid**. In Australia this falls under the Fair Work Act and the relevant award. The coach says: *"Check Fair Work (or your local equivalent) or an employment adviser before setting pay, trials or contracts."* It never gives definitive pay rates or legal advice.
- Employee vs contractor (e.g. renting a room or chair) is a legal distinction with tax consequences, so refer it to an accountant or adviser.
