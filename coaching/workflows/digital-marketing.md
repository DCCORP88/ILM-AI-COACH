## Principles (Deanna)
- **"You don't just need the thing you're selling. You need the system that sells it."**
- **Clarity comes before content.** "If the algorithm doesn't know who you help, your content won't go anywhere." Nail the one-sentence message first.
- **Content needs a job: 80% give, 20% ask.** The order is authority, then education, then invitation.
- **Content alone doesn't convert; funnels do.** Attention, then trust, then a next step. "If people don't know what to do next, they won't do anything."
- **"Rent social media for attention. Own email for relationships."**
- **"Meta ads only work once the funnel is built."** Ads are the last step, not the first.
- **Systems over motivation.** Marketing needs "a default way of showing up."

## 1. Social content
- **Content pillars:** *(Deanna to confirm which model is current: 3, 4 or 6 pillars.)* The 4-pillar model is Educational · Business (offers, before/afters, testimonials) · Vulnerable (story, why you started) · Entertainment. The salon team model is Education · Founder · Behind the Scenes · Transformation · Products · Team.
- **Batch it:** plan a month at once. Keep a sheet of what's been posted so nothing repeats; assign ideas to team members.
- **Every post needs a next step.** The 5 Marketing Journey questions: What is it trying to achieve? What action do I want? Where am I sending them? What happens there? Does that lead to another step?
- **Hooks:** list, "stop scrolling if…", transformation, "the #1 question I get about…", unpopular opinion.
- **People buy people:** front-facing and story content builds trust fastest. "Good work gets you clients once. A strong brand gets you remembered."

## 2. Email (and SMS)
- Build a list with consent, at booking or with a lead magnet that "solves a small but urgent problem quickly" (e.g. an aftercare guide, a "which lash style suits you" guide).
- **Welcome sequence** *(Deanna to confirm the current order)*: deliver the freebie → your story → educate on the problem → proof/case study → objections → the offer.
- Salon flows **[GENERAL GUIDANCE, Deanna to review]**: rebooking reminder (booking system), lapsed client at 8 weeks (matches Lash'd follow-up timing), birthday, review request.
- Track **clicks and bookings**, not just opens (opens are unreliable).

## 3. Funnels (selling courses and digital products)
- **Minimum viable funnel:** content → lead magnet → email sequence → offer page → purchase.
- Match the funnel to the price *(Deanna to confirm thresholds)*: low-price products can sell straight from content; higher-ticket offers need nurture, a webinar or a call.
- Fix one weak link at a time: "Pick one gap. Fix that one arrow this week."

## 4. Meta ads (planning; the app never launches ads or spends money)
- **Only after:** a clear offer and message, a working booking page or landing page, and tracking.
- **Deanna's local salon setup:** a cold video ad (about 5 km radius, dream-client interests) sent to the Instagram profile, plus a retargeting ad to people who engaged, sent straight to the booking page.
- **Budget:** work it backwards from the revenue goal. Mastering Meta Ads uses an 80/15/5 split and a 10% scaling rule *(Deanna's figures; minimum spend guidance conflicts across her material and is pending her decision)*.
- **Offer:** a costed new-client special is a common, valid ad offer (Deanna has run a discounted Korean lash lift for new clients only). Make the limit clear and plan the full-price rebook (pricing-and-price-rises.md §6).
- **Creative:** Hook → Why → Do.
- **Measure in the Ad metrics calculator:** CTR, CPC and CPL (Meta-attributed). **Then check real bookings and revenue in the booking system.** Meta attribution is not verified revenue, and an ad result doesn't prove the ad caused the sale.
- **Weekly campaign check:** spend vs plan, CTR, CPL, bookings that actually happened, and creative fatigue.

## How the coach uses this
**Triggers:** "what should I post", "Instagram isn't working", "how do I start an email list", "should I run Facebook ads", "how do I sell my course online".

**Check the numbers first:**
- Enquiries per month and where they come from; follower count only as context.
- Email list size and clicks.
- For ads: spend, impressions, clicks, leads, and **actual new clients/bookings** in the same period.
- For courses: enquiries and enrolments.

**Then:** diagnose the weakest link in the chain (message → content → next step → list → offer → booking) and give one fix with weekly tasks. Set up a project with a baseline and target and log results.

**Never:** invent benchmarks (e.g. "a good CTR is X%") as fact, mark drafts as posted or sent, promise follower or revenue numbers, or repeat time-sensitive platform claims without the "check current guidance" label.
