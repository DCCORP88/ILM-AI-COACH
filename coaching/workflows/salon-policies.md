## The principle

> Set up your policies and stick to them from the start. You'll look more professional, and people won't take advantage of you.

The most common beginner mistake is **bending policies out of fear of losing clients or money**. That fear is misplaced. Every bend teaches clients to expect leniency, and they stop respecting your time and your policies. Once that habit sets in it's very hard to reverse; often the only clients who respect the policy are new ones who never knew you as a pushover.

Strict policies are not the same as no flexibility (see "Leniency where it matters" below). The difference is that **you** choose when to be flexible, and the client doesn't expect it.

## Deanna's policy set (Lash'd Eyelashes)

| Policy | Lash'd rule |
|---|---|
| **Deposit** | 50% deposit, taken at booking, for **all** services. |
| **Rescheduling** | Free up to **48 hours** before the appointment. |
| **Cancellation inside 48 hours** | Deposit becomes non-refundable. |
| **No-show** | **100%** of the service is charged to the card on file. |
| **Late arrival** | More than **10–15 minutes** late: shorten the service, or rebook, in which case the client pays for the missed appointment. |
| **Payment** | Paid **in full at the appointment**. No "pay later", no PayID after they leave. |

### Leniency where it matters
The no-show and late-cancellation fees exist for:
- clients who **repeatedly** break the policy, and
- **new clients** you don't know yet, where you have no idea why they didn't turn up.

For a **loyal regular** who always attends and has a genuine last-minute emergency, Lash'd **waives the fee**. That's a deliberate choice that builds the relationship. The client knows the policy applies, and you've shown understanding when it counts.

### Telling clients and enforcing it
- Policies appear on the **website**, in the **consultation form**, and in the **waiver / digital contract** the client signs when booking through the booking system (Lash'd uses Fresha).
- To enforce a policy, **refer the client back to the waiver they signed** when they booked. Keep it calm and factual. The policy is the bad guy, not you.

### On payment
> You're not their friend, you're a business. Nobody walks into Kmart and pays later.

- Take payment before the client leaves, every time.
- Chasing money after the appointment causes problems, eats your time and leads to burnout.
- You're providing a luxury service. You shouldn't have to argue to be paid for it.

## How the coach uses this

**Typical triggers:** "clients keep cancelling", "I get no-shows", "should I take deposits", "a client won't pay", "how strict should I be", "I'm scared I'll lose clients".

**Check first** (from profile/project; ask only what's missing):
1. Do they have written policies now? Where do clients see them?
2. Do they take deposits? How much?
3. How often do no-shows or late cancellations happen (per week/month), and roughly what does that cost? (If they don't know, help them estimate it; never invent a figure.)
4. Does their booking system support deposits, card-on-file and signed policies?
5. What's stopping them from enforcing it? (Usually the fear of losing clients.)

**Then:**
- Share Deanna's policy set as a **starting template** (Deanna approved, 16 Nov 2026), not a law. The owner can adjust percentages and windows to suit their services and market.
- Tackle the fear directly, using the principle above.
- Help draft: a policy wording for the website and booking page, a waiver line, and a short enforcement message.
- For the booking page and signed waiver, point owners to their booking system's built-in policy templates (Fresha and most booking platforms include them). The coach's main value is the **words the owner says to clients**, so always offer the client-facing scripts below.
- Next action: put the policy live in the booking system this week. Measure: no-shows and late cancellations per month, before and after.

**Limits:** deposit and cancellation terms must also comply with consumer law, which varies by country and state. The coach adds: *"Check your wording meets your local consumer law. Fair, clearly disclosed terms are the safe approach."* Card-on-file charging depends on the booking and payment provider's rules.

### Example enforcement message — [PROPOSED wording, in Deanna's style]
> "Hi [name], sorry we missed you today. As per the booking policy you agreed to when you booked, missed appointments are charged in full. I've processed that now. When you're ready, you can rebook here: [link]."

### Example policy wording — [PROPOSED]
> **Deposits:** A 50% deposit secures every booking.
> **Changes:** Reschedule free of charge up to 48 hours before your appointment. Inside 48 hours your deposit is non-refundable.
> **No-shows:** Missed appointments are charged in full.
> **Running late?** We can't extend your appointment. After 15 minutes we may need to shorten your service or rebook you, and the missed appointment is charged.
> **Payment:** Full payment is due at your appointment.
